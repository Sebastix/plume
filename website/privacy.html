feather

Privacy

Feather has no analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs.

On your device

The app stores your public account key, signer app name, your media-cache size preference, and bounded caches of public events and media. Feather does not import or store your private Nostr key. Login uses the Android signer you choose. Login requests your public identity. When you tap a post’s heart or select an emoji from its long-press picker, Feather requests that your signer sign that kind-7 reaction. Recent and frequent suggestions are computed locally from your own public reaction history. Feather also asks your signer to decrypt your own private mute list so blocked accounts stay hidden. Decrypted account keys are cached only in private app storage and are never published; private bookmarks and other encrypted lists are not decrypted. No encryption permissions are requested. Up to 256 public signed reactions and their delivery status are stored locally so manual retries can reuse the same event. Android backup is disabled for app data.

On Nostr

Automatically selected seed relays and relays discovered from public NIP-65 relay lists receive your IP address and the requests needed to load notes, profiles, follows, highlights, reactions, and zap receipts. Feather also queries relay.zapstore.dev to discover app feeds and apps published by profiles. Home feed category discovery uses your existing following scope, or public activity when browsing as a guest. Feather publishes a reaction only when you tap a post’s heart or select an emoji and your signer approves. These public reactions include your public key and the referenced post and author. Feather does not publish notes, replies, or follow lists. Relay connections pause when Feather is backgrounded.

Media

Profile pictures and inline images load automatically from their hosts. Visible videos autoplay muted. The app preloads bounded portions of nearby videos before they enter view to reduce playback delays. Those hosts receive your IP address and the requested media URL. Media is cached on your device within the storage budget you choose in settings; Android may reclaim cached files. No Feather media proxy is used. Saving an image writes a copy to Pictures; saving a video writes a copy to Movies. Sharing creates a temporary local copy and grants the receiving app access. Temporary video exports use separate storage bounded to the smaller of the selected cache budget or 512 MiB. Android 8–9 asks for storage permission only when saving. Playback and media requests stop in the background.

Link previews

Visible web links fetch page metadata directly from the linked website, and preview images load from the image hosts it specifies. Those hosts receive your IP address and the requested URL. Feather does not run page scripts or send browser cookies or your Nostr identity. Preview metadata and images share the local media cache. Requests stop when a card leaves view or the app backgrounds.

YouTube previews

Notes containing YouTube video links load thumbnails from YouTube image servers. Visible cards also request the video title and channel name from YouTube’s oEmbed endpoint. These services receive your IP address and the video ID. Responses are cached locally; Feather does not send your Nostr account key or note ID. Tapping a preview opens the original link in YouTube or your browser.

Zap amounts

To validate public zap receipts, Feather fetches the recipient’s Lightning provider metadata in the background. The provider receives your IP address and the requested public payment-address URL. Feather does not request invoices, make payments, or send your private keys. Provider requests stop when the app is backgrounded.

Blocktime

Only when you toggle a note timestamp to Bitcoin block height, Feather queries mempool.space with the timestamp. The service receives your IP address and that timestamp, but no note ID or account key. Successful timestamp-to-height mappings are retained in local app data without expiry so the same timestamp does not require another network lookup. Clearing app data or uninstalling removes them. Lookups stop in the background.

External links

Links open in your browser or another installed app. Those services have their own privacy policies.
