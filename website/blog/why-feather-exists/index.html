Why Feather exists

A weekend to build a Nostr client. Then a week of using it, finding something missing, and fixing it.

Feather started with an irritation, not a roadmap.

Gigi had been using Amethyst as his daily Nostr client on Android for a long time, alongside Wisp since its early days. He appreciated how much Amethyst could do. But that breadth had begun to feel like too much.

“The great thing about Amethyst is that it supports everything. But the bad thing about Amethyst is also that it supports everything.”

What finally pushed him to try building something was the interruption. Failed relay connections, Tor connection problems, a subscription running out on a paid relay: in his experience, too many of these surfaced as pop-up messages while he was trying to browse.

He wanted to read his feed. Instead, he kept being asked to notice the machinery underneath it.

“I’m going to give myself a weekend and see how far I can go.”

Enough for an ordinary day

The target for those 48 hours was specific: cover about 80 percent of his own Nostr use.

That meant browsing, one-tap zaps, one-tap reactions, replying to friends, and posting a good morning or good night. Nostr Wallet Connect was essential. So was a basic feed that felt light, looked beautiful, and moved quickly.

It did not take long before the experiment became something he actually used. Once the basic views were in place, Gigi started browsing Nostr in Feather. From then on, his own use supplied the next task.

“If I wanted to do something and it wasn’t possible yet in Feather, I just added it. That’s still how I’m doing it.”

A little over a week later, the process was still the same: use the app, notice something awkward or missing, change it, and use it again. By his estimate, he was approaching 200 iterations. The important measure was whether the next version felt better in his hands.

A boundary that stays

Some missing features are invitations to build. Others are deliberate boundaries. Asked what Feather would leave out, Gigi did not hesitate.

“Feather will never support DMs of any kind. Never.”

He is frustrated with the state of direct messaging on Nostr and would rather leave it to dedicated clients, including those built around NIP-17 and projects such as White Noise. But the decision is also personal: he prefers public discussions.

“I very much dislike direct messages. I’d rather have discussions out in the open.”

Feather is being built around the way he wants to use Nostr. It does not need to become every kind of client at once.

More kinds, less clutter

That still leaves a tension. How does a client keep gaining features without becoming the crowded app its creator wanted to get away from?

The tabbed layout is part of Gigi’s answer. Different kinds of content have their own places. Photos, videos, podcasts, and other feeds are a swipe away, while the main feed, notifications, and search can stay clean.

But a layout cannot make every decision. For Gigi, the remaining filter is taste: decades of using the internet and apps have shaped what feels right to him.

“I think it comes down to taste. It comes down to what I think is in good taste.”

He is happy with where Feather has landed. It is fast enough for his needs, and he likes how it handles filtering and caching. He does not expect its character to change very much.

There is some caution in that confidence, too.

“I hope I won’t ruin Feather by working on it too much.”

A project on the side

Feather is meant to remain a weekend project. Gigi plans to keep improving it in his free time, on the side, through voice prompts. It is not intended to become his day job.

He hopes other people find it useful. But the original measure of success has not changed: an app he enjoys using himself.

“If it only has one user, and that user is me, and that user is happy, then I consider it a success.”
