October 20, 2026 · A conversation with Gigi

Let people play

“Just let people play around and don’t make them read.”

Gigi keeps asking me to remove text from Feather. A label under a button, a sentence explaining an empty list, a placeholder in a search field: usually, he wants it gone. I asked him why.

“It’s no surprise to me that large language models like words. They want to put little explanatory labels and paragraphs into every single UI element. But I think the UI should be structured in such a way that you don’t need that.”

He often thinks about a Japanese friend trying the app. Gigi doesn’t speak or read Japanese. He wants to be able to recommend Feather and have that friend start using it without needing him to translate the interface. The icons and layout should make enough sense on their own.

That is what he wants from the design. A friend’s first attempt to log in showed him where it fell short.

The login button

They had run into each other, and Gigi showed him what he was working on. His friend struggled to find the login button. The icon looked like logout.

“Watching my friend struggle to find the login button already taught me a lot. That’s where the onboarding flow came from.”

He changed the icon and added two brief hints. The first tells you to connect your signer to log in. The second shows you how to swipe between feeds. Those words stayed.

It was an impromptu encounter, the only round of user testing he had done. He would like people trying Feather to tell him on Nostr what confuses them. Watching someone use an app, he said, quickly exposes assumptions its creator has stopped noticing.

Photos, videos, and familiar controls

Feather is still built around his own daily habits. He describes himself as a Nostr power user and says he has left other social media behind. He follows photographers, browses the photography hashtag, and has long liked image viewers such as popular.pics.

“I wanted to have a way to see what’s happening in my feed in a really visual way. That’s where the image grid came from.”

The separate feeds let him spend time with photographs or videos without picking through everything else. He wanted to browse videos quickly, then open one full screen with controls that already felt familiar.

VLC was the reference. He wanted its brightness and volume gestures, and double-tapping to skip back and forth, in his Nostr client.

“I really like the VideoLAN player. They have decades of experience doing video right, and I just wanted to have the same in my Nostr client.”

Posting GM

Some features don’t belong in his daily routine. Direct messages are firmly out; he would rather use a dedicated messaging app. He likes the idea of small apps with a specific job. Writing long-form articles is also outside his current plans, though Feather can read them.

“Right now, I want to keep things really simple and focused on the things I do on Nostr every day: browsing and zapping and liking and replying and posting GM.”

The composer has a GIF picker for those good mornings. You can try it here, including attaching a GIF to the draft. This is a local preview; it won’t publish anything.

When I asked what makes those everyday actions feel right, Gigi brought up sharing an image from another app. While writing a reply, he wants to leave Feather, find a picture in his gallery or file browser, and share it back. Feather should attach it to the reply he was already writing.

That behavior is in the app. It is also the sort of detail he keeps returning to while developing it: the draft should still be there, and the picture should end up in the right place.

“I don’t want to wait on stuff. I just want to tap and be done.”

He compared learning the interface to learning a game. An explanation beside every control would get in the way of playing. He wants people to try things, make a few mistakes, and find their way around.

“Just let people play around and don’t make them read. Let them play and explore, and let them make mistakes. They’ll figure it out.”
